## Start working — simple view (v0.8.0)

Double-click **Launch Studio.cmd** or press Godot's F5. IsoCity now opens with the city filling the window and a small tool dock. Your existing projects, world layouts, recovery history and favorites continue to work.

1. **Build** opens a visual collection drawer. Pick Buildings, Nature, Interiors, Transport, People, or Details; choose an item and click the city, or drag its card into place. Selecting an item closes the drawer so you can concentrate on placement. **Keep placing** is optional.
2. **Refine** reveals collection and style filters and every variation. Lighting/palette variants are grouped by default, and individual character action clips stay behind variations or an explicit search. Searches can find content across styles. Any item can still be placed in either world.
3. Click a placed object to move it or reveal its action card. Copy, remove, paint, or choose **More options** for detailed transforms. Eligible buildings offer **Edit inside**. Room editing has visible **Back to city**, floor selection and floor-management controls.
4. **Roads** provides drawing, crossings, removal and simple road-size choices. **Nature** gathers landscaping and atmosphere. **City life** organizes residents, travel, businesses, services, budgets, challenges and growth into focused choices. Active budget, challenge and cooperative status remains visible without opening the editor.
5. **Explore** takes you into your place; **Return** brings you back. **Photo** opens the camera tools and keeps the main HUD out of the shot. Key hints follow your remapped controls. Focus mode hides the HUD and provides a visible return action.
6. Use the top **Save** button or **Menu → Saved places**. **Menu → Share your creation** opens sharing. **Menu → Starter scenes** offers layouts; replacing the current world can be undone. The world selector chooses separate Pixel and 3D layouts, both saved in the project.

The full editor is available through **Menu → More tools → Precision workspace**. **Back to simple view** returns without changing your document. The interface choice and welcome dismissal are remembered. Detailed exports are under **Menu → More tools → Export options**. Controls and accessibility settings are under **Menu → Settings & help**.

The simple view uses a warm cream-and-green theme, original pictograms, large primary targets and uncluttered city space. It adapts the collection drawer for smaller windows and retains enlarged text, high contrast, keyboard focus and remappable controls. Scene artwork is not restyled by the UI theme.

## Precision workspace reference

These controls describe the full editor, available behind the simple view:

1. Choose **Small city block**, **Furnished room**, or **Blank template**, then click **Start from template**. Undo can restore the layout you replaced.
2. Browse thumbnail cards, select a category, or search by name. The **All asset types · 2D + 3D** filter lets either world use both libraries; choose **2D sprites**, **3D models**, or **Added packs** to narrow it. Click a card, then place it on the canvas; releasing the mouse clears the asset and returns to **Select / move**. Dragging a card onto the canvas also places it once and clears the cursor. Enable **Repeat placement** explicitly to keep a clicked asset active for consecutive placements. You can paint a stroke by holding the mouse down; releasing ends the stroke. Click **☆** to keep a favorite; favorites persist between sessions.
3. Use **Place**, **Select / move**, or **Erase**. Select an object to drag it, change its height or size, rotate, duplicate, or remove it. **R** rotates; **Ctrl+D** duplicates; **Delete** removes; **Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y** undo and redo. A paint stroke is one undo step.
4. **Snap to grid** aligns placements; turn it off for free placement. The **Brush** paints 2D ground/floor tiles in widths of 1, 3, or 5. WASD/arrows or middle-drag move the building camera. Right-drag orbits in 3D, the wheel zooms, and **Home** resets the view.

   **City Ground and floor rotation:** choose the tile and press **R** or **Rotate** to turn it by the configured step before painting (90° by default). To rotate an existing ground tile, choose **Select / move**, click that tile, then use R or the exact **Angle / yaw** field. Its direction is saved and exported, and rotation supports undo/redo. Ground artwork turns in the isometric plane. Uniform surfaces can look the same after rotation. **Source view** chooses supplied sprite views; the angle field can also rotate single-view artwork.
5. **Walk around** or **Tab** switches to character exploration. The persistent **WALK MODE** label lists its controls, and **Back to building** or Tab returns to editing. Editing panels and shortcuts pause while walking. WASD/arrows accelerate smoothly; Space jumps in 3D. **X** recovers to a nearby clear standing position. The 3D character is an animated block-style person and automatically steps over curbs and stairs up to 0.35 world units. Press **E** near a marked building entrance to visit its own furnished interior, or near a supported door/gate to open or close it. At the interior exit, press E to return outside. **Edit this room** opens that building's room in the normal editor; **Back to city** returns to its exterior. Each building retains its own edits in project saves and scene exports.
6. **Save** writes both layouts into one `.isocity.json` project. **Projects → Open file…** restores that editable project; **Save As…** saves a separate copy with its own recovery history. Studio autosaves each project separately every five seconds after changes and on a normal close, and resumes the last project's autosave at launch. Favorites live in `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\IsoCity\`; project recovery lives beneath `studio_history\projects\` there.
7. **Export scene** writes the active layout as a `.tscn` scene, including its ground, objects, lighting, camera, and playable character. The default destination is `creations/` in this project. Open the exported scene in Godot and press **F6**; Tab switches between walking and free camera. Keep the `.isocity.json` too so you can continue editing in Studio.

When editing a building's interior, **Export scene** and **Portable ZIP** include the city and every owned floor. You can export from connected interior Walk mode without leaving that visit.

**Portable ZIP** exports the active layout as a complete Godot project. Extract it into a new folder, open `project.godot` with Godot 4.7 or later, let the source assets import, and press **F5**. The ZIP includes runtime scripts, sprite views and animations, model sources, paint textures, ground, owned interiors and the furniture for unvisited buildings. 2D packages use Compatibility; 3D packages use Forward+ (Mobile on mobile) for photo depth of field. You can export while walking inside a room without leaving that visit. A dependency manifest records file checksums and asset IDs; supplied asset license files remain at their original paths. `city-source.json` preserves the exported layout for reopening in IsoCity Studio, with the other layout empty.

**Export scene** references this project's assets and scripts. **Portable ZIP** includes those dependencies and Godot settings. These exports produce Godot source projects/scenes; building a standalone game executable uses Godot's export tools. Architectural models and selected props use their actual mesh collision; other assets retain footprint/box colliders or explicit catalog walk profiles.

## Mixing 2D and 3D assets (v0.5.0)

You can place any of the 6,555 indexed assets in either Studio world. Use the asset-type filter above the category list; changing it shows all categories for that type. The world selector still changes which layout you are editing.

- **3D models in 2D** render from an isometric camera with a transparent background. Models with mesh collision project geometry in the character's body-height band, keeping arch openings and spaces under benches passable. This approximates 3D clearance on a 2D walking plane; other models retain footprint colliders. Rotate changes the actual model's heading; size, height, sorting and selection use the 2D world.
- **2D sprites in 3D** appear as upright flat objects facing the camera, with their original directional artwork and animation. City Ground and Floors become flat ground surfaces; their diamond artwork maps onto square floor planes and supports quarter turns. Objects use boxes or authored walk profiles, including cylinder trunks for base tree sprites and thin door shapes. Sprites remain flat when you orbit the 3D camera.
- Mixed placements support previews, movement, rotation, duplication, groups, assemblies, layers, undo/redo, project saves and scene export. Ground sprites placed in 3D are individual selectable objects. The **Brush** continues to paint tiles in the 2D world. Each world remembers its asset-type filter.

The original asset files are preserved. Exported mixed scenes regenerate the appropriate rendered model or sprite object when opened; keep the referenced builder scripts and source assets with them.

## Precise rotation, colors and lane alignment (v0.6.0)

**Full rotation:** choose an asset or select a placed object, then use the **Selection** tab. **Angle / yaw** accepts any heading from 0–360°. **Pitch** and **Roll** tilt models in either world and sprite cards in 3D. **Rotate step** controls the increment for R and Shift+R; try 15° or 1° for small adjustments. **Reset / Alt+R** clears all rotation axes. **Source view** chooses the supplied artwork for directional sprites. At the default 90° step, Rotate still cycles the familiar sprite views; the exact angle field rotates the artwork itself. Flat sprites do not acquire unseen sides. Rotations survive undo, duplication, groups, assemblies, saves and exports.

**Compass rotation (v0.6.1):** select a 3D model, then use the **N / NE / E / SE / S / SW / W / NW** buttons in **Selection**. These turn the actual model in either world and preserve its pitch and roll. Directions follow the screen: in the default isometric view, the additional 45° turns provide N, E, S and W between the diagonal headings. In 3D, the presets follow the current camera after orbiting. The active direction is highlighted; custom angles remain available. Set **Rotate step** to **45°** to cycle through all eight headings with R.

**Per-piece colors:** click **Color / parts**, then click the piece on the canvas. The **Colors** tab shows the selection. Choose a color and click **Apply color to selected piece**. **Connected piece / region** follows touching triangles or pixels of similar original color; lower **Color tolerance** to keep the selection more specific. **Single triangle / pixel** gives exact control. Mesh selections are highlighted on the canvas, and sprite selections have a highlighted image preview. Sprite edits affect the picked source view and animation frame. **Restore this object's colors** restores only that object's original appearance. Every application can be undone; other instances and source files retain their colors. Painting also works on models rendered in 2D, sprites placed in 3D, and painted 2D ground tiles.

**Center cars:** with **Car lane snap** enabled, new cars and individually dragged cars align their visible centers with the road lanes. This accounts for model/sprite origins and scale. Painted pixel roads derive lane centers from the actual asphalt width and marking rows, including wider streets with double markings. To repair existing cars, select them and click **Align cars** above the canvas. With no selection it aligns all visible, unlocked cars near a road in the active world. Alignment is undoable. Explicitly setting a rotation preserves that heading when placing, so lane snapping does not override a deliberate angle.

**Grid alignment (v0.7.0):** the complete indexed library contains **6,555 assets: 5,936 sprites with 48,159 views, and 619 models**. Original authored anchors are retained; imported rectangular bases are measured, flat surfaces are projected onto the 2:1 grid, and irregular silhouettes use an explicit contact anchor. Sliced terrain and block tiles recover their original source-cell padding, so partial edge pieces retain their phase. Default imported footprints occupy whole cells. Model snapping uses vertices near the ground rather than roof overhangs or arbitrary source pivots. New placements and single-object drags snap their bases; **Selection → Snap base to grid** repairs selected objects, or all visible unlocked objects when nothing is selected. Road vehicles retain lane centering. For an adjoining Brutalist row, **Align buildings** joins the footprints along a common front edge. Both commands support undo.

Modular canal and terrain families also recover their shared source canvas, preserving the relationship between cropped bends, arcs and edges. Opening an older layout compensates for changed anchors so existing art keeps its visible ground contact, then snaps editable bases to the nearest grid intersection. The upgrade runs once and creates a **Before library grid alignment** recovery snapshot.

**Paint tiles onto pieces (v0.7.0):** choose **Color / parts**, pick the desired connected region or individual triangle/pixel, then open **Colors → Choose tile…**. Search the tile library; enable **Include all 2D artwork** to use other sprites as patterns. Set the tile size, rotation and X/Y offsets, choose **Artwork / Roof / Left wall / Right wall** for sprite regions, and click **Apply tile to selected piece**. Models automatically map the pattern onto each selected face, including meshes without UVs. This works for both object types in either world and for painted ground. Paint is clipped to the selected piece, preserves sprite transparency, supports undo/redo, and survives saving and export. **Restore original appearance** clears that object's color and tile paint.

## Import custom assets

Open **Import assets** in the inspector or search Commands for **Import custom sprite or model asset**. The wizard has four steps:

1. **Source:** choose a PNG, JPEG or WebP sprite, or a GLB/glTF model. Enter its name, category, author, license and optional source URL. glTF buffers and textures must be available beside the source as referenced by the model.
2. **Geometry:** set the placement anchor and footprint. Sprite anchors and ground-contact coordinates use image pixels; model anchors use source coordinates. Model source scale is applied before placement. Sprite footprints use grid cells; model footprints and collision height use ground units. Disable **Solid footprint collision** for a decorative asset that people can walk through.
3. **Preview:** inspect the source and blue ground footprint. Click a sprite to set its pivot, or return to Geometry for exact coordinates. Models can be viewed from several directions. **Reset source anchor** restores the suggested image bottom-center or model base-center anchor.
4. **Review:** **Review thumbnail** generates the library image. Choose **Import asset** to prepare the asset and select it for placement. The library switches to the **Custom imports** pack.

Imported sprites and models work in either city view. Prepared scenes, textures, thumbnails and attribution are saved under `assets/custom_assets`; the custom catalog is `resources/catalog/custom_registry.json`. Keep these with the project. Source files remain unchanged and are no longer required to use a prepared import. Importing the same source and settings reuses its asset identity; different geometry or metadata creates a separate asset. If a source or glTF dependency changes while the wizard is open, choose the file again before importing.

Portable exports include used custom assets, their thumbnails, attribution and a catalog containing their descriptors. The wizard supports source files up to 128 MiB, images up to 8192 pixels per side, and integer footprint dimensions from 1 to 64. Preparing assets requires a writable IsoCity project folder.
